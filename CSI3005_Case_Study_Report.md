## Executive Summary
In high-volume manufacturing environments, unplanned CNC milling stoppages cause severe productivity losses, tool breakage, workpiece scrap, and occupational safety hazards. Traditional maintenance approaches oscillate between rigid calendar-based schedules (which replace healthy tools prematurely) and reactive firefighting (operating tools until catastrophic breakdown).

This case study establishes an end-to-end interactive visual analytics and condition-based monitoring dashboard using **Streamlit**, **Plotly**, and **Scikit-learn**. By evaluating 10,000 operational machine cycles from the AI4I 2020 dataset, this study demonstrates problem framing, visual defense, graphical integrity critique and correction, continuous streaming adaptation, and predictive modeling with zero target leakage.

## 1. Task 1: Frame the Problem

### 1.1 Operational Problem Statement
Milling tools operate under intense rotational speeds and cutting torque loads that generate severe friction heat. Maintenance teams must balance tool utilization against the risk of catastrophic in-cut tool failure. The primary challenge is transforming multi-parameter telemetry into intuitive visual representations that enable shop-floor engineers to detect impending failure modes prior to equipment stoppage.

### 1.2 Target Audience & Stakeholders
* **Primary Audience — Factory Maintenance Engineers & Reliability Specialists:**
  * *Characteristics:* High domain fluency in mechanical units (Nm, rpm, Kelvin), requiring unambiguous, high-contrast visual cues to prioritize immediate shop-floor physical inspections.
* **Secondary Audience — Plant Operations Managers & Production Supervisors:**
  * *Characteristics:* Require macro-level visibility into failure distributions across product grades (`L`, `M`, `H`) to guide tooling procurement and preventive maintenance capital budgeting.

### 1.3 Decision Objectives
1. **Condition-Based Inspection Prioritization:** Identify machines operating in high-stress torque-speed envelopes or deficient thermal gradients.
2. **Data-Driven Tool Replacement:** Establish empirical tool wear replacement cutoffs rather than relying on arbitrary time estimates.
3. **Root-Cause Failure Diagnosis:** Determine whether impending failure stems from Heat Dissipation (`HDF`), Power boundary violation (`PWF`), Overstrain (`OSF`), or Tool Wear fatigue (`TWF`).

### 1.4 Dataset Attributes & Physical Scope
The study evaluates the **AI4I 2020 Predictive Maintenance Dataset** (UCI Repository):
* **Sample Count:** 10,000 instances
* **Ground-Truth Breakdown:** 9,661 nominal cycles (96.61%), 339 failure cycles (3.39%)
* **Key Monitored Attributes:**
  * $T_{air}$ [K]: Ambient room temperature (mean: 300.0 K, std: 2.0 K)
  * $T_{process}$ [K]: Tool-workpiece friction temperature (mean: 310.0 K, std: 1.5 K)
  * $\omega$ [rpm]: Spindle rotational speed (1,168 to 2,886 rpm)
  * $\tau$ [Nm]: Cutting torque load (3.8 to 76.6 Nm)
  * $t_{wear}$ [min]: Tool contact duration on milling insert (0 to 253 min)
  * `Machine failure` $\in \{0, 1\}$: Binary ground-truth breakdown indicator

> **Dataset Provenance & Fidelity Note:** AI4I 2020 is a synthetic dataset generated by Stephan Matzka (HTW Berlin) to simulate physical milling wear equations. While its thermodynamic and mechanical stress boundaries mirror real machining physics, it is not raw sensor logs collected from a physical factory. Original values are strictly preserved without synthetic fabrication.

### 1.5 The Two Core Analytical Questions
1. **Analytical Question 1:** *How do rotational speed and torque relate to recorded machine failures?*
   * *Engineering Importance:* Spindle motors deliver mechanical power ($P = \tau \cdot \omega$). Determining whether failures occur across the entire speed-torque curve or exclusively at extreme operating boundaries enables engineers to define safe CNC feed-rate programming limits.
2. **Analytical Question 2:** *How does tool wear differ between records with and without machine failure?*
   * *Engineering Importance:* Cutting inserts progressively wear over contact duration. Engineers must verify whether tool wear exhibits a distinct distributional shift during failures to establish safe tool replacement thresholds.

## 2. Task 2: Choose and Defend Principal Visualizations

### 2.1 Principal Visualization 1: Spindle Speed vs. Torque Scatter Plot
* **Visual Encodings:**
  * X-Axis: Spindle Rotational Speed [rpm] (Continuous)
  * Y-Axis: Spindle Cutting Torque [Nm] (Continuous)
  * Color Encoding: Emerald Green / Blue (Nominal) vs. Crimson Red (Machine Failure)
  * Shape Encoding: Circle (Nominal) vs. Diamond (Failure) for color-blind accessibility
  * Hyperbolic Boundary Lines: 3,500 W (lower) and 9,000 W (upper) spindle power limits
* **Analytical Finding:** Failures do not occur randomly across the operating space. They cluster at two distinct hyperbolic boundaries:
  1. *Top-Left Cluster (Speed < 1,350 rpm, Torque > 60 Nm):* Catastrophic tool overstrain (`OSF`) and heat dissipation failures (`HDF`).
  2. *Bottom-Right Cluster (Speed > 2,500 rpm, Torque < 15 Nm):* Low-load power failures (`PWF`) where power drops below 3.5 kW.
* **Defense Against Alternative Chart (2D Binned Heatmap):**
  * *Alternative Considered:* 2D rectangular density heatmap of speed vs. torque counts.
  * *Why the Scatter Plot is Superior:* In this dataset, failures represent only **3.39%** of observations. A 2D density heatmap aggregates counts into bins dominated by the 9,661 normal operating cycles, completely submerging and hiding rare failure events. The scatter plot with an explicit foreground z-order for red diamond marks ensures that critical failure events remain immediately visible.

### 2.2 Principal Visualization 2: Tool Wear Distribution by Failure Status Box Plot
* **Visual Encodings:**
  * X-Axis: Machine Operational Condition (Categorical: "No Failure" vs. "Failure")
  * Y-Axis: Tool Wear Duration [min] (Continuous)
  * Marks: Median line, Interquartile Range (IQR) box, 1.5× IQR whiskers, dashed mean line, outlier points
* **Analytical Finding:** Machine failure records exhibit a markedly higher median tool wear (**~148 minutes** vs. **~105 minutes** for nominal operations). However, failures also occur at early tool life (< 50 min), demonstrating that tool wear alone is not the sole failure cause—thermal dissipation deficits and power spikes cause premature failures regardless of tool age.
* **Defense Against Alternative Chart (Grouped Bar Chart of Averages):**
  * *Alternative Considered:* Grouped bar chart comparing mean tool wear (107.7 min vs. 143.7 min).
  * *Why the Box Plot is Superior:* A bar chart of means collapses the rich distributional spread into single numbers, hiding variance, skewness, and early-life failures. Maintenance engineers need to know the minimum wear at which failures begin (lower whisker and outliers), not merely the average. The notched box plot preserves the five-number summary and reveals the true operational spread.

## 3. Task 3: Critique and Correct Misleading Visualizations

### 3.1 Critique of Flawed Visualization
*(Insert Screenshot from Page 4: Task 3 Flawed Chart)*

* **Flaw 1: Truncated Y-Axis (Baseline Disanchored):**
  * *Description:* The Y-axis is artificially constrained to start at **106.5 minutes** and end at **108.5 minutes**, omitting the zero origin ($0\text{ min}$).
  * *Distortion:* Across product variants, actual mean tool wear is virtually identical (Type L: 107.9 min, Type M: 107.7 min, Type H: 108.6 min—a negligible 0.8% difference). The truncated axis visually exaggerates this 0.9-minute variance into an apparent 400% height difference, misleading managers into believing Type H tools wear out four times faster.
* **Flaw 2: Misleading / Reversed Colour Semantics:**
  * *Description:* Arbitrary traffic-light colors (Green for Type L, Yellow for Type M, Red for Type H) are assigned to nominal product types.
  * *Distortion:* In industrial analytics, green universally signifies "Safe" and red signifies "Critical Danger". Painting Type H red triggers false alarms, while painting Type L green falsely implies complete safety (despite Type L having the highest failure count in the plant).

### 3.2 The Corrected Visualization & Improvements
*(Insert Screenshot from Page 4: Task 3 Corrected Chart)*

* **Correction 1: Zero-Baseline Anchoring ($0\text{ to }130\text{ min}$):** Honoring the physical zero origin accurately communicates that tool wear contact duration is uniform across all product lines.
* **Correction 2: Neutral Accessible Palette:** All bars use uniform technical blue (`#3B82F6`), eliminating false hazard associations.
* **Correction 3: Statistical Error Bars (95% Confidence Intervals):** Explicitly communicates that minor variances between variants are within expected sampling variation and not statistically significant.

## 4. Task 4: Continuous Data Arrival Adaptation

### 4.1 Real-Time Streaming Architecture
When transitioning static charts to an active continuous condition monitoring environment:
1. **Update Interval & Polling Frequency:** Recommended **1.0 second per dashboard refresh**. Spindle sensors generate data at ~1 kHz; edge PLCs aggregate raw waveforms into 1 Hz peak-and-mean packets to match human supervisory visual absorption limits without browser DOM thrashing.
2. **Display Time Window:** Rolling **50 to 100 historical cycles** (approx. 2–5 minutes of operational context) managed via a fixed-size double-ended queue (`collections.deque(maxlen=100)`), preventing browser memory exhaustion during multi-week production shifts.
3. **Dual Aggregation:** Discrete raw telemetry points are plotted to capture instant impulse shocks, overlaid with a **5-point moving average** to reveal underlying thermal and torque trendlines.
4. **Sudden-Change vs. Long-Term Drift:**
   * *Sudden Changes:* Handled via threshold triggers ($\tau > 60\text{ Nm}$) and rate-of-change ($\Delta\tau/\Delta t$) interrupts that fire immediate visual alert banners.
   * *Long-Term Drift:* Monitored via cumulative tool wear slope, forecasting Remaining Useful Life (RUL).

### 4.2 Limitations of Historical Replay
1. **Passive Observation:** Replaying recorded CSV rows cannot simulate human-in-the-loop interventions (e.g., operator reducing feed rate).
2. **Artificial Regularity:** Historical rows arrive with uniform timestamps, omitting real-world network packet jitter and PLC clock drift.
3. **Fixed Failure States:** Historical breakdown cycles are predetermined and cannot be averted by test interventions.

## 5. Machine Learning Predictive Pipeline & Anti-Leakage Audit

### 5.1 Strict Anti-Leakage Policy
To prevent falsely optimistic 100% accuracy that collapses in production, the classification pipeline strictly excludes:
* `Machine failure` (ground-truth label)
* `TWF`, `HDF`, `PWF`, `OSF`, `RNF` (specific failure modes known only at or after failure)
* `UDI` and `Product ID` (non-generalizable identifiers)
* `Heuristic Risk Score` (derived target proxy)

## 6. Engineering Conclusions & Maintenance Recommendations

1. **Implement Dual-Boundary Spindle Interlocks:** Configure CNC machine controllers to restrict operation above 60 Nm cutting torque when spindle speed drops below 1,350 rpm to eliminate Overstrain (`OSF`) and Heat Dissipation (`HDF`) failures.
2. **Dynamic Tool Replacement Threshold:** Establish tool replacement alerts at **190–200 minutes** of contact wear, avoiding both premature disposal and catastrophic tool breakage.
3. **Coolant Flow Monitoring:** Ensure heat dissipation gradient ($\Delta T = T_{process} - T_{air}$) remains strictly above **8.6 K** through proactive coolant pump maintenance.
4. **Graphical Integrity Mandate:** Ensure industrial dashboards maintain zero-baseline representations on ratio-scale metrics to prevent costly misinterpretations by plant leadership.
